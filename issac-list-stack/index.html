Part 1 — What actually built the list

No scraper was used. Nothing was bought. Six data sources, all public, all free, read directly. That is why the list beat ColdIQ's paid agent 37,182 rows to 20.

The six sources in the order they ran

  • 1 · Texas Comptroller open data — Socrata API on data.texas.gov, datasets jrea-zgmq (sales-tax permits) + 9cir-efmm (active franchise tax). One API call, ~90 seconds, $0. Gives: legal name, NAICS, county, street address, org type, year first traded, number of locations, taxpayer number.
  • 2 · Dallas Central Appraisal District (dallascad.org) — read account by account, by hand. Gives: owner of record, deed date, land value, improvements value, BPP value, exemptions, tax agent, annual tax bill. This is the layer that produced the five.
  • 3 · The companies' own websites — history page, About page, officer names, phone, published email.
  • 4 · Better Business Bureau + Dun & Bradstreet — principal names and titles, cross-check only.
  • 5 · RocketReach — email pattern inference only. Produced the Garland Steel guess at 68.6% confidence, which was flagged and not sent.
  • 6 · Web search + news — founding stories, generations, acquisitions, obituary-free background. This is what caught Dymatize being sold to Post Holdings for $400M in 2014 while ColdIQ ranked it Priority A.

The funnel 239 to 5

885,461 TX sales-tax outlets
→ 37,182  in 27 counties, in-ICP industry, 20+ years trading, single location
→ 13,293  at 30+ years  ·  5,201  tier-A industry (the A-list)
→ 239  narrowed to Dallas County only
→ 17  taken to the county property record one at a time
→ 5  the operating company is the recorded owner of its own building

Twelve failed. Ten own the machines but rent the dirt (Manda Machine — the parents own the land personally). Two have no DCAD record at all. Roughly 1 in 5 passes.

The scoring model max 85 · automated

  • Trading age — 10 to 30 pts  ·  Sector tier — 5 to 20  ·  County tier — 7 to 10
  • Single location — 15 (94% of the file; single location = owner-operated)  ·  Has a corporate entity — 8 to 10
  • Everything after scoring was done by hand. The property check, the officer names, the emails — none of it is automated yet.

The pipeline was never saved as a script. The Comptroller pull was written inline and thrown away. The CSVs exist; the code that made them does not. Right now the list cannot be re-run, re-filtered, or pointed at Tarrant or Collin County without rebuilding it from scratch.

Part 2 — The signals to layer on

Industry, county and revenue are what everyone filters on. They tell you a business exists. None of them tell you the owner is ready. These do — and every one is readable from the sources already in Part 1.

A · The disengagement clock when did he stop caring

  • ChaseBPP value falling year over year. DCAD publishes multiple years. Machines depreciating with no new purchases = the owner stopped reinvesting. This is the financial fingerprint of "I'm done" and almost nobody looks at it.
  • ChaseHe stopped protesting his property tax. DCAD shows the appointed tax agent. An owner who fought the valuation for 15 years and quit has checked out.
  • ChaseReview replies stopped in year X. Google Business Profile timestamps it exactly. So does the last Facebook post and the copyright year in the website footer.
  • ChaseHiring stopped. No careers page, no posting in 18 months. Harvesting, not growing.
  • OpenerA recent review saying "not what it used to be" or complaining about lead times. Decline is when owners sell.

B · The succession vacuum sharpened version of the fix

  • DropA new officer with the same surname. Heir seated. Out.
  • ChaseA new officer with a different surname. He hired a professional manager instead of a son. Owner stepping back, no family heir — this is the perfect seller, and it is the exact inverse of the signal above from the same field.
  • ChaseThe kids are on LinkedIn doing something else. Doctor in Houston, dev in Austin. No heir, confirmed from a public profile.
  • ChaseHis own LinkedIn title changed to Chairman, Advisor or Consultant. The step-back already started.
  • ChaseHis home address moved out of county. Public record. Running it remotely from the lake = semi-retired already.
  • ChaseOver-65 exemption on his home. Confirms owner age 65+ from a record instead of guessing off the company's founding year.
  • ChaseThe 30-year office manager or controller is retiring. Losing the person who knows where everything is, is what actually forces a lot of these sales.

C · Paperwork that only moves before a transaction

  • ChaseRegistered agent changed recently — often the first thing a lawyer does when prepping a deal or an estate plan.
  • ChaseA second entity registered at the same address by the same officer — usually a holding co or a real-estate LLC formed ahead of a sale.
  • ChaseFranchise-tax forfeiture then reinstatement. Nobody is minding the back office. Also means the books need cleaning before sale — which is work Issac can charge for.
  • VerifyRegistered agent is a law firm, not the owner. Someone is already advising him. Could be a competing broker. Owner-as-agent = open field.
  • VerifyThe property already sits in a family trust or a separate LLC. Estate planning is done — but the dirt may not come with the business. Check before promising a double commission.

D · The dirt where the money actually is

  • ChaseLand worth more than the buildings. The building is obsolete, the dirt is the asset, and the owner usually doesn't know it. Cary Products is exactly this — $1.4M of land under $931K of buildings.
  • Chase40,000 sq ft, 12 employees. Over-housed = the operation shrank. Sell the business, sell the excess dirt.
  • OpenerDeed held 30+ years. Fully depreciated, huge capital-gains overhang. He needs someone who understands a 1031 or an installment sale — that is Issac's credibility line in sentence one.
  • ChaseRezoning, opportunity zone, or a new highway/DART line adjacent. The dirt reprices without him doing anything.
  • CarefulPlating, metal finishing, foundry, dry cleaner. Environmental liability. A Phase II ESA can make that property a cost, not an asset — this inverts the whole double-commission thesis on specific NAICS codes. Bass & Hays Foundry is on our failed list already; know this before pitching one.

E · Human, and legal to use

  • OpenerThe anniversary year. A 50th or 75th is a natural moment to think about what comes next, and a warm, non-creepy reason to write. Southern Maid is 89 years old. Timing beats copy.
  • OpenerRotary, church board, trade-association past-president. He has already moved his identity out of the business — and it is a warm-intro path, which beats any cold email we can write.
  • AngleThe building carries the family name, or the About page is written in first person. He will only sell to someone who keeps the name. Changes the pitch, not the score.
  • ChaseCompany equipment listed on used-machinery marketplaces. Liquidation already started.
  • ChaseDropped off the trade-show exhibitor list after year X. Same clock as the reviews.

F · Deal quality — know before he walks in

  • Price itUnder ~$5M is SBA 7(a)-financeable. That means individual buyers, a deep pool, a fast close. Above it you are bidding against private equity. This is why Republic at $11M+ is a two-year name and Thermo Sensors is a 30-day one.
  • Price itOne logo on the homepage. Customer concentration destroys the multiple. Better to know before the valuation conversation.
  • Price itUnion vs non-union. Changes the buyer pool completely.
  • OpenerThe annual property tax bill. Public, exact, and something he feels every January. Garland Steel pays $85K a year. That is a first line no list broker can write.
