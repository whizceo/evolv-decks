The starting point

What the account does today

Nine facts about the account as it stands. They are listed only so the build on the right is legible — every one of them is closed by day three.

Only paid contacts flow inMeta lead forms write to the CRM automatically. The website forms and the intake sheet do not.
Records live in three placesThe CRM, a Google Sheet, and JotForm — none of them reconciled to each other.
Tags carried over from the old setupNo naming convention, so a tag can't be trusted to mean anything.
Two of three automations don't runOne has no trigger. The prescription-form automation never fires.
No deal pipelineThere is no stage a contact sits in, so the funnel exists in someone's head.
No alert when a contact arrivesFirst touch happens whenever an inbox gets checked.
Nothing decides who is called nextPriority is memory, not a list.
No sending authenticationWhether the email reaches the inbox has never been verified.
Nothing goes back to the ad accountMeta has never been told which contact actually bought.
The point of all of it

Nothing above is a people problem. It is configuration — which means three days of work, not a rebuild.

01Day

One source of truth

Data, fields and structure. Every enquiry that has ever reached CytoKind ends the day in one place, described the same way.

01.1
Audit and freezeFull export, every existing tag, list and automation mapped to what it actually does. Nothing is deleted before it is understood.
01.2
One intake, six doorsMeta lead forms · the four website forms · the n8n intake sheet · JotForm · Calendly · inbound email — all writing to the same contact record.
01.3
Backfill the missing historyThe intake sheet and the website history imported and de-duplicated against the contacts already in the account. Everyone who ever reached out becomes reachable.
01.4
The fields the business actually runs onCondition · Indicated yes/no · Who pays · Insurer · Prescriber and NPI · Device · Prescription status · JotForm status · Source, campaign and creative.
01.5
A tag architecture, five familiesSOURCE_ · CONDITION_ · STAGE_ · ACTION_ · OUTCOME_. Anything that doesn't belong to one of them is retired.
01.6
Six segments that get used every dayIndicated · Not indicated · Never contacted · Prescription outstanding · Booked · Customer.
02Day

The engine

Pipeline, routing, speed and follow-up. The day the software starts doing the work instead of storing it.

02.1
A deal pipeline, seven stagesNew → Contacted → Qualified → Prescription sent → JotForm complete → Ordered → Delivered & invoiced. Every contact sits in exactly one, and moving is automatic.
02.2
Routing at the moment of submitIndicated goes down the insurance track and is told, immediately, that their cost may be $0. Not indicated goes down its own track. The form decides, not a person.
02.3
Speed to lead, enforcedNew contact fires an SMS and a push inside 60 seconds carrying the name, the condition and the phone number. The six-minute standard stops being a promise and becomes a setting.
02.4
Call tasks that create themselvesEvery new contact generates a dated task. Three attempts — day 0, day 2, day 5. Anything unworked escalates. Nobody is called twice and nobody is missed.
02.5
The three automations rebuiltTrigger, condition, exit and goal on each one, tested with live contacts before it is switched on.
02.6
The outstanding-prescription sequenceThe people who have already said yes and have not yet signed, chased automatically until the JotForm is in. The single highest-value automation in the account.
02.7
Follow-up cadence written into the softwareNot a free-text date in a field that notifies nobody — a scheduled sequence with an owner and a deadline.
02.8
Lead scoringCondition, insurer and engagement combine into one number, so the call list is ordered by who is most likely to buy today.
03Day

Measurement and handover

Proof the machine works, and the ability to run it without us in the room.

03.1
DeliverabilitySPF, DKIM and DMARC on the sending domain, reputation warm-up and an inbox-placement test. From day three, whether an email arrives is a known number.
03.2
Site and event trackingTracking live on cytokind.net — form starts, form abandonment, article reads, page depth. What people do before they submit becomes visible.
03.3
Attribution, end to endCampaign and creative stamped on the contact at capture and carried all the way through to the invoice.
03.4
Conversions sent back to MetaEvery completed sale uploaded to the ad account, so the platform starts optimising toward buyers instead of form-fillers.
03.5
One dashboard, five numbersContacts in · time to first touch · connect rate · JotForms completed · devices invoiced. Open it and you know where the business is.
03.6
HandoverAutomation health check, a one-page owner's manual, and a 90-minute working session so the team can run and change it themselves.
Day zero — what we need from you
Three things, and the clock starts
  • Admin access to the ActiveCampaign account.
  • Access to the website forms and the DNS record for the sending domain, so authentication and tracking can go live.
  • Forty-five minutes with whoever works the phone, to confirm the pipeline stages and the fields before anything is built.
