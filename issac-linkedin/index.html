Part one

The decision

Eight lines that define the whole of phase 1. If a thing is not on this list, it is not being done.

  1. The objective of phase 1
    Find the sentences that make a 62-year-old Texas business owner stop scrolling. Nothing else is being attempted, measured or optimised for sixteen weeks.
  2. Why only that
    Everything downstream — the offer, the lead magnet, the funnel, the newsletter, the video — is built on top of a hook that works. Build any of it first and you are decorating a guess.
  3. The instrument
    OWNER REACH. One number, taken from the post's own analytics panel. It scores the hooks.
  4. The indicator
    QUALIFIED OWNER REPLY. One number, counted by hand on a Friday. It judges the strategy.
  5. Everything else
    Ignored for ninety days. Followers, likes, impressions in aggregate, comments from other brokers, connection requests, profile views. Not tracked, not reported, not discussed.
  6. Who writes
    Issac. First person, always. A man who has been in the room, not a firm with a point of view.
  7. What is not in phase 1
    No lead magnet. No newsletter. No video. No carousel. No link. No booking page. No call to action beyond a plain sentence. No follow-up sequence. No automation on the account.
  8. How long before it means anything
    Four weeks to a first read on the territories. Sixteen weeks to a decision. The account will look like it is doing nothing for the first three of those weeks. It is not.
Part two

Why this happens on LinkedIn

Not because it is a marketing channel. Because of four specific things about this man.

Follow-up sequences kill him, and content does not

DATAThe most fatal line in the whole psychographic: the second automated message proves the first one was automated. Email forces you to choose between one shot and being found out. Content removes the choice. You publish, you never chase, and the waiting is done by the work.

He looks you up before he replies, not after

READ'Be useful first, for months. He will look you up himself.' That look-up is the actual conversion event in this market, and the profile plus the last twenty posts is what he finds. The account is not a channel. It is the thing the cold email is checked against.

He can read without being seen reading

READThis man has carried the decision alone for two to six years specifically so that nobody knows he is thinking about it. A post can be read at eleven at night with nobody notified. A reply to an email is a disclosure. That asymmetry is the entire reason this works.

It is the only place recognition scales

DOCOne verifiable fact about his company is what starts a conversation — and it does not scale. Recognition of his situation does scale, and it is the next best thing. LinkedIn is where one sentence can be true about four hundred men at once without being generic.

The audience is small, so precision beats volume

DOC494,881 businesses in the 27 priority counties, of which the real target is a few thousand men. A post that reaches 800 of the right people beats one that reaches 40,000 of the wrong ones, and on LinkedIn you can see which one happened.

Part three

One instrument, one indicator

The instrument ranks the hooks. The indicator judges the strategy. Everything else is ignored.

The original engine note said the metric is stop rate — impressions to a meaningful dwell. That is the correct concept and it is not directly measurable on LinkedIn. LinkedIn does not expose dwell time or expand rate on a personal profile, and it never has. Building a sixteen-week test on a number that cannot be read would have wasted the sixteen weeks. So the concept is kept and the measurement is replaced with the closest thing the platform actually reports.

The instrument — Owner Reach

  1. What it is
    OWNER REACH = post impressions × the share of viewers LinkedIn reports as Owner / Founder / President / CEO seniority. Rounded to the nearest ten. One number per post.
  2. Where it comes from
    The post's own analytics panel. Impressions is the headline number; the seniority and location splits are two taps down under 'Top demographics'. Two minutes per post.
  3. Why impressions are a legitimate proxy for stopping
    LinkedIn's ranking system expands a post's distribution when early dwell is high and suppresses it when it is not. Impressions are the observable shadow of dwell. You cannot see the cause; you can see what the cause produced.
  4. Why the seniority filter is not optional
    Raw impressions on a broker's account are dominated by other brokers, coaches and advisors. That traffic is real, it is worthless, and it will happily make a bad hook look like a good one. Multiplying by owner share removes the entire failure mode.
  5. How a hook is scored
    Owner Reach Index = this post's Owner Reach ÷ the median Owner Reach of the trailing ten posts. Above 1.3 = the territory keeps its slot. Below 0.7 = it is a candidate for the cull. The first ten posts have no trailing median, so they set the baseline and score nothing.
  6. What it is not
    It is not engagement, it is not a like count and it is not a leading indicator of revenue. It is a ruler for hooks. That is the whole of its job.

The indicator — Qualified Owner Reply

  1. What it is
    QUALIFIED OWNER REPLY (QOR) — one comment or one direct message from a person whose LinkedIn profile shows an owner-level title at a Texas business in the target trades.
  2. What counts
    Owner, President, Founder, Partner, CEO, General Manager-with-equity. Texas. The business is real, is at least five years old, and is not a brokerage, a fund, an advisory or a marketing company.
  3. What does not count
    Every other broker. Every coach, consultant, fractional anything, lender, insurance man and search-fund associate. Anybody outside Texas. Anybody who liked it and said nothing.
  4. How it is counted
    By hand, once a week, on a Friday, into one line: week / QOR count / who. No dashboard. Building a dashboard for a number that will be 0, 0, 0, 1, 0, 2 is theatre.
  5. Why this is the only indicator
    One reply from a 64-year-old machine-shop owner in Rockwall County is worth more to this business than four hundred impressions from the industry, because one seller listing is worth six figures. The economics of the business make every other number a distraction.
  6. What it will be for the first month
    Zero. Probably zero for six weeks. That is expected and it is not a signal. The instrument steers the ship; the indicator tells you whether the ship is going anywhere. You need both, and only both.

Ignored for ninety days

  • Follower count — Grows fastest when you post things other brokers enjoy. It is a measure of the wrong room.
  • Likes and reactions — Almost entirely peers and vendors in this segment. A 62-year-old owner reads and leaves.
  • Comments from the industry — Warm, well-meant, and they will pull the account toward writing for brokers within a month.
  • Total impressions — Un-filtered reach flatters bad hooks. Only Owner Reach is looked at.
  • Profile views — Not attributable to a post, and not stable enough to steer by.
  • Connection requests — Inbound requests in this market skew heavily to service providers selling to Issac.
  • Anything on a daily basis — Nothing here is read more often than weekly. Daily checking produces decisions on noise.

Part five

Three types of post

The house system runs four. Phase 1 runs three, and the fourth is set to zero on purpose.

40% of posts · awarenessRECOGNITION

Say out loud the thing he has never said. No teaching, no advice, no fix. The whole post is “I know what this is like and I am not going to make it weird.”

It is the only content that reaches a man at stage 1, because he does not have a question yet. He has a feeling he has never heard described.

Territories — THE SECRET · THE DAY AFTER · THE KIDS AREN'T COMING · THIRTY YEARS IN THE SAME BUILDING

30% of posts · storytellingTHE ROOM

One deal, one moment, one thing Issac watched happen. Anonymised to the point of being unfindable. One story, one insight, and the insight is never the moral of a fable.

This is the single asset no competitor can copy and no AI can invent: twelve closings in 2025 and a decade of rooms. It converts 'a broker' into 'the man who was there' faster than any credential.

Territories — Any territory. The story is the delivery mechanism, not the subject.

30% of posts · educationalMETHOD

How the thing actually works, given away with nothing asked. The four drivers of a multiple. Why the dirt prices differently to the business. What a buyer's accountant does in week three.

'Be useful first, for months.' Method content is the proof of competence that lets a man who distrusts everyone under forty conclude, privately, that this one knows the work.

Territories — THE NUMBER · THE BUILDING · WHAT YOUR CPA ISN'T FOR · DUE DILIGENCE · FOUR IN FIVE DON'T SELL

The house system has a fourth type — Conversion. It is set to zero for sixteen weeks. Not reduced. Zero. Every conversion mechanic available on LinkedIn (comment-a-keyword, lead magnet in the DMs, booking link, results screenshot) trips at least one item on the trust-killer list for this specific buyer.

Part six

The test design

Twelve territories, forty-eight posts, sixteen weeks, one variable.

  1. The unit under test
    The territory, not the post. Twelve territories, already defined and already sourced from the psychographic. A territory is a claim about what this man cares about.
  2. The bank
    12 territories × 4 hooks = 48 posts. Four posts is the minimum that tells you about a territory rather than about a Tuesday.
  3. Cadence
    3 per week. Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday. Mid-morning Central. Fixed for the whole sixteen weeks. Cadence is not a variable in this test.
  4. Duration
    16 weeks. 48 posts at 3 per week. Weeks 1–4 are the first block and set the baseline.
  5. Order
    Territories are interleaved, never blocked. No territory posts twice in the same week, and no two consecutive posts share a shape. A territory that ran three Tuesdays in a row would be measuring Tuesday.
  6. The one-variable rule
    Within the sixteen weeks, exactly two things vary: the hook (the thing being tested) and the image (balanced, so it cannot contaminate the hook read). Length is fixed to two shapes. Everything else is frozen.
  7. The floor
    A post below 0.7 Owner Reach Index is a miss. Three misses out of four in a territory kills the territory at the next review, with no discussion.
  8. The review
    Every four weeks. Rank the twelve by median Owner Reach Index, kill the bottom four, write four new hooks inside the winners. Arithmetic only. No opinions in the review.

Part seven

Two shapes, locked

Length is not one of the things being tested, so it is frozen to two options for the whole run.

60–90 wordsTHE SHORT

Architecture. Hook. Rehook. Three or four single lines. Flat close.

Used for. Recognition, and the sharp end of Method. Anything that would be weakened by explaining it.

Why it is measurable. It is fully readable inside the feed without expanding, which means it is the cleanest possible test of whether the sentence itself did anything.

220–280 wordsTHE LONG

Architecture. Hook. Rehook. A turn. Three or four beats. A line that lands. Flat close.

Used for. The Room stories and full Method pieces. Anything where the value is in the detail.

Why it is measurable. Long enough that expanding it is a real decision, which is exactly the behaviour the instrument is reading.

The engine note specified ~60 and ~250 words. The short shape is widened to 60–90 and this is a proposed amendment, flagged rather than made silently. At sixty words a LinkedIn post does not reach the 'see more' fold at all, which removes the expansion signal entirely — and the expansion signal is a large part of what drives the distribution the instrument reads. 60–90 keeps the post short enough to be one idea and long enough to be measurable. Issac's call.

Part eight

The caption law

The rules every post obeys. They come from the house caption framework and from the trust-killer list, and where those two disagree the trust-killer list wins.

  1. The two lines are the whole test
    He sees line one, line two, and the image. Nothing else, until he decides. Line 1 = the hook. Declarative, specific, never mysterious. Line 2 = the turn. It flips, deepens or costs something. Both must clear the fold.
  2. One blank line between everything
    Single lines only. No paragraph blocks. A wall of text is a scroll-past in this segment before a single word is read.
  3. Sentence length: max 14 words, varied
    The house rule is eight. Eight is right for Whiz and wrong for Issac — at eight words a 60-year-old broker reads as a 25-year-old doing a LinkedIn voice. Fourteen with variation sounds like a man who has quoted jobs on the back of an invoice.
  4. First person, always. Never 'we'
    The entire strategy rests on him being a specific man rather than a company. A corporate voice forfeits the only advantage he has.
  5. Flat, plain, declarative
    No exclamation marks. No superlatives. No emojis anywhere, including the close. No em dashes. No hashtags. Enthusiasm reads as a young person selling, and nothing ages a message faster here.
  6. Tenure, never age
    'Thirty-one years in the same building' says everything 'at your age' would say, with respect. One reference to his age and the account is finished with him.
  7. Never a number about a specific business
    Statistics about the market are fine and are cited. A number about a business he could identify is not. He checks things.
  8. The close is a sentence, not a call to action
    No 'comment BUYER below'. No 'DM me'. No link. No booking page. No 'thoughts?'. End on the strongest plain line in the post and stop. If a question is used, it must be one he would answer in his own head rather than in the comment box.
  9. No urgency, ever
    'Whenever, or never' is a stronger close in this market than any deadline. This is the one rule with no exceptions and no clever workarounds.

Part ten

The image question

Ten posts, five with a card and five without, and an honest account of what that can and cannot tell us.

There is a real conflict to resolve here. The engine note says text-only, because an image makes it impossible to tell whether the hook or the picture did the work. That is correct. It is also true that image posts and text posts are distributed differently by LinkedIn, and running sixteen weeks of text-only means never finding out which one this audience actually stops for.

  1. You cannot A/B test on organic LinkedIn
    There is no split-test tool. Two versions of one post cannot be shown to two halves of the same audience. Anyone who says otherwise is describing paid ads. So the test has to be built into the schedule instead of into the platform.
  2. The design: balanced, blocked on territory
    Every territory ships exactly half its posts with an image and half without. Across the first ten posts that is 5 and 5, one of each in every territory. Territory therefore cannot explain a difference between the two groups, which is the entire point.
  3. The image is one variable, not five
    One fixed image system for the whole test: a plain typographic card. Off-white ground, one line of the post's own text set in the serif, small monospace caption bottom-left, thin rule. No logo, no photograph, no icon, no colour, no branding. Identical treatment every time. The moment the images start differing in style, the test is measuring art direction.
  4. How it is read
    At the four-week review: median Owner Reach Index of the five image posts against the five text posts. Ten posts is a first look, not a verdict. The question is answered at post 48, not post 10.
  5. If the hook read and the image read disagree
    The hook read wins. The image is the secondary variable and it is balanced precisely so that it can be ignored if it turns out not to matter.

The card specification

  • Canvas — 1200 × 1200, square. Squares take more feed height than landscape on mobile.
  • Ground — #FDFDFB off-white. Never white, never dark, never a gradient.
  • Type — One line, Newsreader or any transitional serif, 400 weight, roughly 84px, ink #16181A, set at 1.15 line-height, ragged right, generous margins, optically centred.
  • Caption — Bottom-left, JetBrains Mono 400, 22px, #6B6F72. Never a name, never a title, never a URL.
  • Rule — A single 1px #E3E1DB hairline above the caption. Nothing else on the card.
  • Forbidden — Logos. Photographs. Stock images. Headshots. Icons. Colour. Quotation marks. Drop shadows. Anything that looks designed.
  • Why so plain — The card must read as a page from a document rather than as a graphic. A designed image on this account signals marketing, and marketing is what he has been deleting for six years.
Part eleven

Before post one

Seven things that must be true about the profile before the first post goes out, because the profile is where the decision actually gets made.

  1. The headline
    It must say what he does for sellers, in his own words, with no adjectives. If it currently reads as a list of services or contains the word 'passionate', it is costing reach on every single post in this test.
  2. The banner
    Plain. Ideally a real photograph of an ordinary Texas commercial property, not a skyline and not a stock handshake. It is the first thing he sees after clicking the name.
  3. The About section
    Written first person, past tense, about work done rather than services offered. Twelve closings in 2025 is a fact; 'trusted advisor' is not.
  4. The featured section
    Empty in phase 1. Deliberately. There is nothing to gate and nothing to sell yet.
  5. Contact
    Direct email visible. A man who has decided to write does not want to use a form.
  6. No automation on the account
    No connection-request automation, no auto-DM, no scheduler that posts on his behalf without him seeing it. One automated artefact on this profile undoes the whole strategy.
  7. Connections
    Do not mass-connect before or during the test. It changes who the algorithm samples with, which changes the instrument, which invalidates every number recorded before it.
Part twelve

The weekly ritual

Fifteen minutes on a Friday, forty-five once a month. That is the entire operating cost of the measurement.

  • Tue / Wed / Thu, mid-morning Central — Post. One post. No editing after twenty minutes.
  • Every day, 0 minutes — Nothing. The account is not checked daily. This is a rule, not a preference.
  • Friday, 15 minutes — Open each of the week's three posts. Record impressions and owner-seniority share. Compute Owner Reach. Count QOR by hand. Three lines in one sheet.
  • Every fourth Friday, 45 minutes — Rank the twelve territories by median Owner Reach Index. Kill the bottom four. Write four new hooks inside the surviving eight. Read the image split. No opinions — the numbers decide.
  • Never — Deleting a post that underperformed. A miss is data and the account is not a portfolio.
