04 — What the numbers say

01The psoriasis ads are not underperforming. They are three versions of the same idea.

All three land between $3.65 and $4.08 per enquiry — a very narrow band. When three variations produce nearly identical results, the variations are not the problem; the underlying idea is at its ceiling. What is being tested is the picture, not the message.

02The MS ad wins on a different idea, not a better execution.

It leads with new research. It brings in enquiries at $2.02 against the psoriasis average of $3.88, and reaches its audience 22% cheaper. That angle has never been tried on psoriasis — which is the one place it would be pointed at buyers who actually purchase.

03The best-funded psoriasis ad is wearing out.

The simple banner takes half the psoriasis budget and has now been seen three times by the average person in its audience, against 1.9 for the other two. It is being shown to the same small group over and over. That is the ceiling of an audience, not of an ad — and it will get more expensive from here.

04Direction has already improved, and it should be said.

In the month to 3 August, 85% of measured spend pointed at MS Fatigue. In this window it is 40%. The account has moved meaningfully toward the buying segment. The remaining 40% is now the open question rather than the whole problem.

05 — What this report cannot tell you

400 enquiries arrived. We cannot yet see how many became sales — and that single number decides whether any of the above is good news.
  1. Sales in this window, split by segment. Six devices were recorded as of 14 August, against a target of twenty by 10 September — today. The current figure, and whether any came from MS, settles the argument on this page for good.
  2. Enquiries from the three paused landing-page campaigns. $1,237.26 bought 3,985 landing-page views there, but the account only counted views, not enquiries. Until that is counted we cannot say whether the landing page or the Facebook form is the cheaper way to buy a customer — and the two are being chosen between right now.
  3. What happens after someone presses submit. Cost per enquiry is only meaningful next to what an enquiry is worth. That is a follow-up question, not an advertising one.

06 — The recommendation

Take the idea that is winning and point it at the people who buy.

Run the “new research” angle on psoriasis as a genuine fourth ad — not another banner variation. It is the only untested combination of the account's best-performing message and its only proven segment, and it costs nothing to find out.

Hold MS spend where it is rather than cutting it, until the sales figures in 05 are in hand. Cutting it now would be a decision made on cost, which is exactly the mistake this report is about.
