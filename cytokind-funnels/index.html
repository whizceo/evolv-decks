▲ Steal for CytoKind
  1. Build /do-i-qualify/ as its own ad destination. Three questions: your condition · do you have a dermatologist · are you insured. It sorts the 82% MS traffic away from the derm traffic before Adrian picks up the phone — the single biggest leak in CytoKind's funnel today.
  2. Build /cost-and-insurance and put paid budget on it. "Insurance pays $0 for you" is the derm story and nobody is telling it. Aetna opened vitiligo coverage July 2026 — that page writes itself.
  3. Run the local webinar as the mid-funnel. Inspire fills a named doctor's room in a named city on a named date. CytoKind's equivalent: a dermatologist-hosted webinar on home NB-UVB, and every attendee walks out with a prescriber.
  4. Stop leading clinically. Only 1 ad in 7 at Inspire is a medical claim. CytoKind's entire library is medical — and it's the reason the ad account is at zero.
  5. The spouse is the second buyer. 35 Inspire ads are the partner's point of view. For psoriasis and vitiligo, the partner and the parent are the same untapped persona.

▲ Steal for CytoKind

▲ Steal for CytoKind
  1. Put a quarter of the budget on dermatologists. Celluma proves an LED device company can run a B2D funnel profitably — and in the last six days they scaled from 65 ads to 103, holding that quarter. CytoKind's prescriber funnel is blocked on one thing: capturing the prescriber as a CRM record — which is exactly what /professional-registration is.
  2. Sell the practice more repeat patients, not a device. "How I turn first-time clients into regulars" translates directly: a dermatologist who prescribes home phototherapy keeps the patient in their practice instead of losing them to an in-office schedule they can't fill.
  3. A device brand is allowed to be human. Their single biggest hook is a song lyric. CytoKind's four hooks are all clinical — and it's why FB pulled a creative for policy language in the first place.
  4. Shop by condition, in the patient's words. /collections/acne → CytoKind's /psoriasis, /vitiligo, /eczema.
  5. Note what they're cutting: the "let me educate you on LED" cluster is down 38%. Education converts after the click, not in the hook.

Why it leaks — the break is one specific moment

"They fill out the form, which is a landing page, and then they hit submit… and that's the end of the experience." — Adrian, 14 Aug. They are returned to Facebook. There is no thank-you page, no education, no routing, no next step, no explanation of what happens now. "The funnel ends after we get their contacts." Everything after that moment is a human being trying to phone a stranger who has already gone back to scrolling — "so we're letting go of them while we have them."
