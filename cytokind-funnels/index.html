▲ Steal for CytoKind
  1. Build /do-i-qualify/ as its own ad destination. Three questions: your condition · do you have a dermatologist · are you insured. It sorts the 82% MS traffic away from the derm traffic before Adrian picks up the phone — the single biggest leak in CytoKind's funnel today.
  2. Build /cost-and-insurance and put paid budget on it. "Insurance pays $0 for you" is the derm story and nobody is telling it. Aetna opened vitiligo coverage July 2026 — that page writes itself.
  3. Run the local webinar as the mid-funnel. Inspire fills a named doctor's room in a named city on a named date. CytoKind's equivalent: a dermatologist-hosted webinar on home NB-UVB, and every attendee walks out with a prescriber.
  4. Stop leading clinically. Only 1 ad in 7 at Inspire is a medical claim. CytoKind's entire library is medical — and it's the reason the ad account is at zero.
  5. The spouse is the second buyer. 35 Inspire ads are the partner's point of view. For psoriasis and vitiligo, the partner and the parent are the same untapped persona.

▲ Steal for CytoKind
  1. The company chases the prescription — not the patient. This is the whole teardown in one line. CytoKind has 14 prescription forms parked = $28,000 waiting on patients to go get a doctor's signature. Aeroflow would have called those doctors on day one.

▲ Steal for CytoKind

The steal board

Eight moves, taken from the six teardowns above, ranked by money moved — not by how interesting they are. P1 = days 1–30, optimise what's already running. P2 = days 31–90, build the system underneath.

1
Chase the prescription yourself

Aeroflow's whole model. 14 parked prescription forms = $28,000 sitting on patients to go get a signature. CytoKind calls the doctors instead. Zero build, this week.

P1 · $28K
2
Build /do-i-qualify/ and put the ads on it

Inspire runs 18 ads at exactly this. It sorts derm from MS before a human is spent, and it's the direct fix for 82% of leads going to a $2–7K cash dead end.

P1
3
Say "$0 — insurance covers it" in the hook

Aeroflow's top eight hooks are all cost. Hers says "$149". Both of CytoKind's closed sales were $0-to-patient — and that sentence appears in no ad.

P1
4
One landing page per condition, encoded in the URL

Hers' /lp/{source}/{category}/{product}. A live psoriasis campaign with no psoriasis page is the cheapest loss on the board — and the URL grammar gives reporting with no analytics build.

P1
5
Name every creative angle_persona

Invisalign's _cost_youngadultfemale. Free, immediate, and the only way the ads seat returns a lesson instead of a CPL. Six angles × three personas = 18 cells to fill.

P1
6
Ship the prescriber funnel with its own registration page

Dexcom's provider coverage tool is its single biggest paid destination; Celluma sends 32% of ads to /professional-registration. The only compounding funnel — and it's blocked purely on capturing the prescriber as a record.

P2 · compounding
7
The local dermatologist webinar

Inspire fills a named doctor's room, in a named city, on a named date, and pays for it. Education plus prescriber-introduction in one event — and it answers "leads are captured before they're educated" at the source.

P2
8
Name the patient identity and film it

~100 Dexcom ads are people saying "I'm a Dexcom warrior." 58 Inspire ads are patient ambassadors. CytoKind has Kathy and two closed derm patients — that's three, and three is enough to start.
