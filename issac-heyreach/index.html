What changes lead to lead

Variables + CSV

HeyReach variables use single braces and are case-sensitive: {FIRST_NAME} works, {{FIRST_NAME}} and {first_name} don't. The three tracks share one copy; the track-specific words are written into the CSV cells, so HeyReach only swaps whole phrases.

VariableTypeSourceRule
{FIRST_NAME}built-infirst_nameMapped from the CSV, not taken from LinkedIn. Title-cased, first name only.
{companyName}customcompany_nameBUSINESS campaign. Short trade name, no LLC / Inc. Same column as the email lane.
{city}customcityBUSINESS campaign. City of the business, as on the Texas record.
{firstYear}customfirst_yearBUSINESS campaign. Year the business started (4 digits).
{street}customstreetPROPERTY campaign. Street name only, no number ("Irving Blvd").
{yearsOwned}customyears_ownedPROPERTY campaign. Whole years owned. Under 5 = row is out.

Before upload (every Monday)

  1. Source: the three C1 lead tables. Take rows with a personal LinkedIn URL (/in/, never a company page) that have NOT been uploaded to Instantly.
  2. Set lane = LINKEDIN on every row you take, in the lead table itself. From then on the email build skips them. One owner, one lane, forever.
  3. Dedupe both ways by person AND company: if anyone at that company is in the email lane, drop the row.
  4. Same suppression as Instantly: DNC (1,526) + Issac's contacts/clients + anyone in GHL. HeyReach can't use a CSV as an exclusion list, so it happens here.
  5. Open 10 at random: right person, not a namesake, still at the company, the column values are true. Drop the batch's bad pattern if you see one.
  6. Order by ICP score, take the top 75 (one week of requests). PROPERTY rows under 5 years owned are out, same as email.
  7. Split by track: BOTH + BUSINESS rows → the BUSINESS file, PROPERTY rows → the PROPERTY file. A blank cell in a column that file uses = delete the row.
  8. BUSINESS file: linkedin_url, first_name, last_name, track, company_name, city, first_year → issac-c1-li-biz-w01.csv. PROPERTY file: linkedin_url, first_name, last_name, track, street, years_owned → issac-c1-li-prop-w01.csv.

Only ~28% of rows carry a LinkedIn URL (14/50 in the actor test). LinkedIn takes the best-scored 75 of those a week; everything else stays in the email lane. 75 a week out of the email pool changes nothing on that side.

Reply playbook

"Sure" / "what do you need?"
Great. Three quick ones and I'll put it together: 1. Roughly what does it do in sales a year? 2. Roughly what do you take home from it? 3. Anything a buyer should know, like a big customer or a lease? Ballparks are fine. It stays between us.
Tag Interested. For PROPERTY: size, occupied or empty, any lease in place.
"What does it cost?"
Nothing. It's a number, not a listing. If you ever sell, you'll know who to call.
Then the three questions.
"How did you find me?"
Fair question. Your company is on public Texas records, and I work with owners in your area. If you'd rather I didn't message again, just say so.
Honest and short. Never mention the tools or the list.
"Not interested" / "not selling"
Understood, thanks for telling me. If it's ever useful, the offer stands.
Tag Not interested. The reply already stopped the sequence.
"Stop" / "don't message me"
(no reply)
Tag Do not contact + add to the blacklist + DNC the same day.
Nothing launches until these are closed
