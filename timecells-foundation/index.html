Pillar 01 · Market Research

It isn't "crypto education."
It's behavioral money skill.

The single biggest decision in the whole strategy is which market we say we're in. The research settled it — and that one choice writes the rule for every post and every funnel.

Context · not the headline
$3.2B
Crypto education · 15–17% CAGR · volatile

Big and fast, but hype-tainted and cycle-dependent. Lead here and we draw the wrong audience and the wrong regulators.

The anchor
$12.8B
Financial literacy · steady 9–10% · →$28.6B by 2034

4× the crypto market, durable and regulator-friendly. This is the category we claim. Behavioral money skill — not market calls.

The edge
$1.3B
AI personal finance · 22% CAGR · fastest

The fastest-growing slice and PAILOT's home turf — the differentiator that makes the training actually stick.

→ Content

Every piece leads with money behaviour & literacy. Crypto is the backdrop, never the headline. AI is the proof of edge.

→ Funnels

Every funnel anchors on literacy; the token / crypto layer only surfaces at the deepest stage (F3), once trust is earned.

Three more findings that build the engine.

Pillar 02 · The ICP

Who we're for —
and the machine we're fixing.

The research opens with the brutal truth the whole content system exists to solve.

The broken machine

"We have never converted a follower into a customer. Zero conversion."

99.5% of revenue flows through an agent hand-selling in a room. There is no working cold path today. The content system and funnels exist to build one — without bypassing the agents who sell.

ICP 1 — The end user ~90%

of our marketing build

The "Operator" — 25–40, male-skew, self-employed / side-hustler. Sees himself as an operator, not an investor. English-first, global; DACH is evidence (22% of German millennials own crypto), not a wall.

ICP 2 — The agent Luco's lane

drives 99.5% of revenue today

The distributor network. We don't disintermediate them — the end-user funnels feed and arm the agents. Our ~10% here = giving agents content and a credible, compliant story to sell with.

The fourth pain is the opening — the one nobody else is selling against.

Pillar 03 · Competitor Analysis

Two competitors draw the map:
one to copy, one to avoid.

We don't have to invent the funnel or guess the risks. The field already shows us exactly what to steal and exactly what to never become.

Finelo — the template app twin · 1M+ users

A near-identical behavioral-finance app already exists and works — $6.99/wk · $19.99/4wk · $39.99/12wk. The product isn't novel; the funnel is proven. So we copy the funnel and differentiate on what they can't build.

Steal the funnel: Landing → 1-min quiz (segment) → account → pick 1 of 3 challenges → risk-free simulator → 3-tier paywall → habit layer (XP / streaks / badges / 28-day challenge).
finelo.com ↗
IM Academy — the landmine $1.2B · FTC + AG

Same shape — education + MLM + token. An FTC + Nevada AG complaint (May 2025) alleges $1.2B defrauded; banned in markets, 1.5★, branded a pyramid. Six sins we invert one-for-one.

The moat: the compliance + transparency spine. Not polish — it's the single thing that keeps TimeCells off IM Academy's road. Transparency shipped as a feature.
iyovia.com (ex-IM Academy) ↗
White space
"Token learn-&-earn"

A real category no one credibly owns — co-ownership + behavioral training + a trustworthy network, held together. We name it and plant the flag.

Steal the persona
Cleo → PAILOT

Cleo won by making finance a character, not a feature. PAILOT becomes a personality the content speaks as — memorable, ownable.

meetcleo.com ↗
Mine for format
Giants' playbooks

Binance's creator army, Robinhood Snacks' 36M-reader newsletter, eToro's social proof — proven formats we adapt, not invent.
