01Method

How this was built

Discovery is the first of six stages. Its only job is to establish what is true — before anybody proposes anything.

Rule 01

Artifact‑anchored, not memory‑anchored

Never “how many.” Always “show me the last five.” Every number here came out of a system that was opened on a call — the CRM export, the ad account, the JotForm, the website — not out of anyone’s recollection.

Rule 02

Nothing is estimated

Where a metric does not exist, this document says it does not exist. That absence is itself the most repeated finding in the file: eleven of the twelve numbers a business like this runs on are not being measured at all.

Rule 03

Priced on the floor unit

All money is calculated on the $2,000 Phothera 200, the cheapest device CytoKind sells. Every dollar figure in all six documents is therefore a minimum, and none of them depends on a margin or ASP figure nobody has had to supply.

The six stages

01 Discovery — what is true today.   02 Data + AI audit — what the systems actually hold and do.   03 Bottlenecks — where the money stops, ranked.   04 Solutions — one fix per bottleneck.   05 Prioritization — impact against effort, every solution placed.   06 Roadmap — the order, the owner and the date.

03The agenda

What discovery set out to answer

Twenty questions, in three groups. Nine are answered. Eleven are still open — and which ones are still open is itself a finding.

Numbers
  • Answered. Contacts in the CRM, sales to date, close rate, prescriptions parked, new contacts per day, phone and email connect rates, drip open and click rates.
  • Open. Total monthly ad spend. MS versus dermatology split. Impressions, CTR, cost per booked call, cost per sale. Form abandonment rate. Monthly site traffic and how much is still the Scientific American article.
Model
  • Answered. Why a patient buys here rather than direct. Cash‑pay versus insurance mix. Whether a telehealth prescriber is possible — it is not, and the reason is structural.
  • Open. What the distribution agreement gives them. Whether the clinical line has any sales motion at all. Reconciliation of units delivered against units invoiced — never performed.
Operations & compliance
  • Answered. Who does what across marketing, creative, ads and sales. Where the CEO’s time goes. What breaks in the automation.
  • Open. Who signs off medical copy. Where patient health data sits and under what safeguards. What happens to a patient after the device ships.
Two questions deliberately not asked

Margin per unit and average selling price. Neither is needed. Every dollar figure across the six documents is calculated on the $2,000 floor unit, which makes each of them a minimum rather than a claim — and removes the one question that would otherwise have to be answered before any of this could be priced.

04Findings

Ten things that are true today

Ordered as they occur in the funnel, not by severity. Severity is stage 03.

F01

The supplier is also the competitor

CytoKind is the US distributor of Phothera NB‑UVB devices. Phothera is the former Daavlin — Daavlin and National Biological merged under Madison Industries and rebranded. That company runs its own direct‑to‑patient request flow, its own dermatologist portal, and advertises broad insurance coverage at $0 cost to eligible patients.

Any plan that only says “convert more leads” without answering why a patient buys here instead of there is built on sand. Discovery found the answer, and it is finding F02.

F02

The traffic is autoimmune. The site is dermatology.

The inbound the business actually receives is overwhelmingly not standard dermatology — MS, long Covid, autoimmune fatigue, post‑viral neuropathy — arriving from a Scientific American cover article. Two of the three homepage testimonials are MS patients. Yet MS sits one navigation item deep, and the site is built for psoriasis, vitiligo and eczema.

This is simultaneously the only lane the supplier does not own, and the only lane with no FDA indication behind it — which is why it is a positioning decision, not a copy tweak.

“I listened to an article in Scientific American and was amazed by the results found in MS patients… I have a family member with severe fatigue, auto‑immune disorder, and autonomic nervous system dysfunction.”
Verbatim inbound enquiry, from the live intake
F03

Insurance is the sale — and it lives only in one person’s mouth

For a dermatology patient with an indication, insurance covers the device and the patient pays nothing. That single fact is the strongest closing argument the company has. It appears in the automated email, and it appears nowhere on the website — no insurance page, no HSA/FSA, no financing, no letter‑of‑medical‑necessity template.

Consequence, observed live in August: some buyers reach the phone still not knowing that insurance covers it. One told the company it sounded like a scam, went and checked the website himself, came back a day later and bought. He rescued himself. Nothing in the funnel does that for anyone else.

“They don’t know what the JotForm is, they don’t know that insurance covers it, they don’t know that they need XYZ from their dermatologist.”
Adrian, 14 Aug 2026
F04

The funnel ends at the submit button

The ads work. The landing page works. Then a patient fills in the form, presses submit — and is returned to Facebook. No thank‑you page, no education, no routing, no explanation of what happens next, no statement of what it costs.

Everything from that second onward is one person on a phone connecting once in fifteen to twenty attempts, against roughly twenty new arrivals a day. Email connects once in forty. The four‑email drip opens at 25% and clicks at 1%.

“They fill out the form, and then they hit submit… and that’s the end of the experience. So we’re letting go of them while we have them.”
Adrian, 14 Aug 2026
F05

Leads are captured before they are educated

The ask comes before the value. By the time a human calls, the person on the other end has no memory of, and no reason for, the conversation. This is not a follow‑up problem — it is a capture‑order problem, and it was reached independently by the CEO and by the person making the calls, ten days apart.

“I’ve talked with many patients on the phone and it’s a matter of: what is this? Oh, I did that form one time. I have no context of what’s going on.”
Adrian, 07 Aug 2026 — “Why don’t we educate them a little further before we ask for the information?” — John, same call
F06

Half the paid spend points at the segment that has never sold

Four ads are live. Two creatives, each run as an A/B pair. One half points at psoriasis, where there is an FDA indication, insurance pays and the patient pays $0 — and where every sale the company has ever made came from. The other half points at MS, where there is no indication, the patient pays $2,000–$7,000 in cash, and zero devices have ever been sold.

The stated reason MS stays live is that it is a benchmark. That is a decision to make, not a system to build — and it is already being discussed internally.

“Most of those are MS. The close rate out of derm is pretty good.”
Adrian, 14 Aug 2026 — the mix is the cap, not the effort
F07

One completed form is one sale. Four have ever been completed.

The JotForm captures the insurance card, the clinical notes and the prescriber’s NPI — everything the manufacturer needs to ship. It is the single unambiguous conversion event in the business. In the entire history of the company it has been completed four times.

Meanwhile eighteen to twenty prescriptions sit parked, waiting on a signature nobody is chasing. Those are people who have already decided.

“If you fill out that form, it’s a sale, basically. We only have four. We do that a hundred times, we do it a thousand times. That’s what we need.”
Adrian, 14 Aug 2026
F08

The fix for the largest leak was built, and never switched on

A dynamic qualification form already exists. It asks where the psoriasis is, what insurance the patient carries, what symptoms they have — and ends by telling them their insurance will cover the device and handing them the JotForm. It has never been tested, never been shipped, and the CEO did not know it existed.

It sat in a folder for weeks while roughly twenty people a day walked into the leak it was built to close. This is not a capability gap. It is an ownership gap.

“I built it and I don’t know why we haven’t put this… I don’t exactly understand why we didn’t pursue it.”
Adrian, 14 Aug 2026
F09

Eleven of twelve numbers do not exist

Form abandonment is invisible — a cost is paid at the click and the drop‑off is never recorded. Sales calls are not recorded, so a close rate cannot be listened to. Two of three CRM automations do not function and the tags are decorative. Fewer than eight hours have ever been spent inside the CRM, and the only goal configured in it was set by the CEO.

The business has also been given two different connect rates that disagree by a factor of 4.6 — the CRM says one thing, the person making the calls says another. Both cannot be used to make a decision.

“If somebody doesn’t fill out the form, we don’t know anything about it.”
Tom (983 Group), 07 Aug 2026
F10

Nobody owns the whole pipe

Ads are set up by one party. Creative is made by another. The landing page by a third. The calls, emails, CRM and the form by a fourth. Every individual seat is filled and every individual seat is doing its job — which is exactly why findings F04, F07 and F08 can all be true at the same time and nobody is at fault for any of them.

The gap is not a person missing from a function. It is that no function owns the distance between an impression and an invoice.

“No one is owning the whole pipe.”
John MacMahon

06Open

What discovery could not close

Each of these is held by a named party. None of them is a research task — they are all one request away.

Ad performance history

Total monthly spend, the MS versus dermatology split, impressions, CTR, cost per booked call, cost per sale. Held by the ads partner; a review was scheduled and arrived without the detail. One report closes it.

Form abandonment

How many people arrive at the form and leave without submitting. Not held by anyone — it has never been instrumented. A cost is paid at the click and the loss is invisible. One tag closes it.

Delivered versus invoiced

Units already shipped to patients, reconciled against units actually invoiced. The reconciliation has never been performed. The figure discussed internally is material and remains unverified, which is why no number for it appears anywhere in these documents. One afternoon and a spreadsheet closes it.

The people who are not in the CRM

The count of enquiries the business believes it has received exceeds the count of contact records by a wide margin. The website form and the intake sheet were never integrated with each other. One import closes it.

Medical copy sign‑off

NB‑UVB for MS and autoimmune conditions is research‑stage, not an FDA‑cleared indication. Every claim in that lane is regulated, and no named person currently approves the wording. One decision closes it.

Where the health data sits

Conditions, medical history and prescriptions are moving through spreadsheets and inboxes. Whether any of that is covered by an agreement, and who can see it, has not been assessed. One assessment closes it.

Numbers

Nothing estimated. Every figure comes from a recorded call or a live system, and all money is priced on the $2,000 device — so each one is a floor.
