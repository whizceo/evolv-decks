02 — The operator

Who he is

The story he tells is the product. It is unusually specific, which is why it works.

His own About copy, in full:

“Nel 2024 sono partito da zero: una mentorship, nessun contatto e non madrelingua inglese. Mi sono ossessionato al processo fino a scalare l'infobusiness americano, diventando top producer su offerte premium nel mercato più competitivo al mondo.”

Three things are load-bearing in that paragraph, and all three are deliberate. Started 2024 — recent enough to be relatable to a 22-year-old. Not a native English speaker — removes the biggest objection his audience has. Top producer in the US market — the status claim that makes him worth listening to.

The second half of the About is where the business actually lives:

“Oggi vendo ancora attivamente. Ma parallelamente gestisco team commerciali, lavoro con aziende per trovare e formare nuovi closer, e aiuto chi vuole costruire una carriera seria nelle high ticket sales.”

So: still closing himself, plus running sales teams, plus recruiting and training closers for companies, plus coaching aspiring closers. Four jobs. That is a founder-bandwidth story before it is anything else — and it is the single most important line in this whole analysis for what we sell him later.

Personal texture

  • Travels heavily and posts it — Miami, Peru, Australia. Lifestyle marquee of 8 photos on the homepage; a YouTube video titled Un Giorno da High Ticket Closer in Perù.
  • Has publicly addressed being called a scammer — YouTube, 23 Jul: “Tutti mi danno della scammer?!” He is aware of the category's credibility problem and is actively fighting it. This is why the Trustpilot profile exists.
  • Posts in the first person, lowercase, unpolished captions (amo la mia vita. / è grato della sua vita.). The brand voice is intimate, not corporate.
  • No meaningful LinkedIn presence found under this identity. For someone selling closers to companies, that is a notable hole.
  • Active on Skool since 2 Dec 2025. Member of STARK — Your Sales Partner ($497) and Poppy AI Viral Content Academy — he buys courses and he is actively studying AI content generation.
Signal

A man who has already paid $497 for a sales community and joined an AI content academy is a man who buys tools and pays for leverage. He is not a hard-sell audience on the concept of paying for systems — only on the price.

The point

He still closes, runs sales teams, recruits closers for companies and coaches beginners. Four jobs, one person. That is a bandwidth story before it is a marketing story.

03 — The business

What the business actually is

On the surface it looks like another closing course. It isn't. There are three distinct businesses stacked on one audience, and only one of them is obviously monetisable.

Layer 1 — The audience engine free

Instagram reels and YouTube shorts in Italian, near-daily, all pointing at a free course. The single repeated CTA across 14 of the last 22 reels: commenta “sales” e ti mando accesso al corso gratuito. Comment-to-DM keyword play. No opt-in page, no email capture, no ad spend.

Layer 2 — The community free

Split across two platforms. Discord (615 members, 47 online) is the link in his Instagram bio. Skool (485 members, free) holds the actual course — Full academy di high ticket sales. His own site describes Discord as “La più grande community di sales in Italia” and Skool as the academy. Weekly live calls, roleplay, Q&A, real call recordings, frameworks he says he paid $20K+ of US mentorships to acquire.

Layer 3 — Placement Hub the real product

A two-sided private network matching closers with companies. Live web app at app.italiancloser.com, Google SSO, separate closer and company logins, closer profile builder, offer feed, interest signalling, company brief intake, dedicated Slack/WhatsApp channel per company, curated shortlists. This is a genuine software product, not a landing page.

Placement Hub — the mechanics, in his words
SideStep 1Step 2Step 3
Closers Build profile — sector, ticket, role, results. Set status In cerca to enter the curated pool. Browse offers, flag interest. Placement stays manually curated. Team sends the profile to the company. Intro only if the fit is real.
Companies Share the brief — funnel, ticket, ideal profile — with a named contact. Get company-area access + a dedicated Slack or WhatsApp channel. Receive a shortlist of 3–5 pre-filtered profiles with direct intros.

The positioning is deliberately anti-marketplace: “Rete privata. Non è un marketplace aperto.” · “Matching manuale. Nessun marketplace.” · “Niente algoritmi opachi: la selezione resta umana e mirata.”

Corrected 21 Aug 2026 — the money exists, and it is buried

The paid tiers were found on 21 Aug, inside Skool. The free course has locked modules gated behind Premium (unlocks OBJECTION HANDLING) and VIP (unlocks all 10 US modules). So the earlier read — “no monetisation exists” — was wrong. The accurate read is worse: monetisation exists but is invisible from every public surface.

No price appears on the site, in the app's public pages, on Discord, on Linktree or in the bio. The only place a visitor can encounter a paid tier is inside the one asset he has personally abandoned — a Skool with 486 members, 0 online and 17 posts in its entire history. The demand side (companies) is still priced nowhere: it goes through a DM and a mailto:.

The point

The money exists. It is priced inside the one asset he stopped showing up to — a Skool with 486 members, zero online and 17 posts in its entire history. The company side isn't priced anywhere at all.

Content pillars, as observed

  • Tactical sales teaching — objection handling, call frameworks, real call breakdowns. His strongest material and the source of his single 22K outlier.

  • Credibility defence — the scammer video. Rare, and clearly reactive.

10 — The unknowns

Open questions carried into Document 02

What the public record cannot answer. These are the hinges the funnel analysis will turn on.

  1. How much does Premium and VIP cost, and does anyone buy? Partially answered 21 Aug: the closer side is monetised through two Skool tiers (Premium, VIP) that gate the course modules — but they sit inside a community with 0 members online and no member post since 31 March. The company side remains unpriced anywhere: a DM and a mailto. Placement fee per hire? Retainer? Rev-share? Still unknown.
  2. Which side is the constraint — closers or companies? He has 1,100 people across two communities and claims only 20+ partner companies. If demand is the bottleneck, everything he is currently doing on Instagram is the wrong work.
  3. What broke between April and August? The comment-to-DM rate fell 110×. Algorithmic suppression of keyword CTAs, an audience gone stale, or a shift in what he's asking for — this needs isolating before anything is fixed.
  4. How much of the delivery is him personally? Manual matching, manual shortlists, weekly live calls, per-company Slack channels, hand-sent DMs, plus he says he still closes actively. Where is the ceiling and how close is he to it?
  5. Is there a team? He writes “il team” and “valutiamo” throughout the Hub copy. One admin online in Skool. Real staff or the founder's plural?
  6. Why no email list? 1,100 community members and zero owned contact data. Deliberate, or never got to it?
  7. Why no LinkedIn? He sells B2B recruitment to companies and has no presence on the only channel where those buyers live.
Headline read, one line

He has built demand-side proof, a working product and a live audience in four months — and has parked the only paid tiers he owns inside the one asset he stopped showing up to. The bottleneck is almost certainly not traffic.
