The finding that matters — read this first

Neither of them is beating CytoKind on marketing.
They are beating it somewhere this instrument cannot see.

The brief assumed Phothera and Solius were running Facebook ads. They are not — zero paid, zero landing pages, confirmed live and historical. The reflex is then to look at their organic and find the playbook there. There is no playbook there either. Both accounts are small, infrequent and barely engaged with. If either company is selling devices, the demand is being created somewhere neither Meta nor this teardown can measure. Here is the honest read of where.

Phothera The threat is distribution, not marketing.

Phothera is Daavlin and National Biological merged under Madison Industries — a forty-year narrowband-UVB manufacturer, not a startup. Its customer is the dermatology practice, not the patient. That demand is created by field sales representatives, distributor and DME relationships, presence at AAD and the derm conference circuit, a separate physician portal at photherahcp.com, published reimbursement and insurance support, and decades of installed base already sitting inside those offices.

A direct-to-patient ad funnel would put them in competition with the exact channel that sells for them. Zero ads is not a hole in their strategy. It is their strategy.

Which means Phothera cannot be beaten with better creative. It is beaten in the derm office — by becoming the device the practice already trusts, or by reaching the patient who then walks in and names CytoKind to their own doctor. That is a channel fight, not an ad fight.

Solius The threat is regulatory, not marketing.

Solius raised $19M and has been publishing for nine weeks. Whatever sales exist are almost certainly coming from the raise itself — founder and investor networks, PR, earned media around the published research it cites, podcast appearances, and a $99.99 Founding Membership sold into a list that existed before the Instagram account did. There is no evidence of a repeatable acquisition engine, and it would be a mistake to assume they have volume at all.

The real danger is structural. Solius obtained a brand-new FDA product code, SGZ, clearing personalized UVB for over-the-counter sale with no prescription and no doctor.

They did not out-market the prescription stage. They deleted it. And that is the precise stage where CytoKind's funnel stalls — 18 prescriptions parked, 4 JotForms completed in the company's history. A regulatory move, not a marketing one.

What this means for CytoKind

The paid patient channel in home phototherapy is genuinely empty, and CytoKind is the only one of the three standing in it. Seven active ads against zero and zero. Nobody is bidding against you, nobody is raising your CPM, and the $8.56 blended CPM in the account reflects that.

But an empty auction is not automatically a cheap one. Nobody is bidding because nobody has proven the patient can be sold to directly in this category — the incumbent sells through the doctor, and the challenger removed the doctor. CytoKind is doing neither. It is going straight at the patient while keeping the prescription. That is a real position, and it is defensible, but it means demand has to be created, not captured.

Everything downstream is priced by that one fact. The submit page, the phone leg, the parked prescriptions — none of it is a leak in a funnel that would otherwise convert. It is the machine that has to manufacture the intent in the first place. Build it as an engine, not as a patch.

Steal from Solius
  1. Publish a thesis, not a product. Every Solius post that broke a thousand views was an argument with a source attached. CytoKind owns a Scientific American article that closes roughly one in three of everyone who reads it — and currently puts it behind an email sequence running a 1% click-through. The asset is right; the placement is wrong.
  2. Put the number in the caption. 15 of 26. The number is what makes a stranger stop, and the citation is what makes them believe it in a category full of $200 Amazon lamps.
  3. Ship the comment gate this week. Comment "COVERED" and we'll send you the insurance checklist. It captures an identity Meta will not give you, costs nothing, needs no build, and works on a small following — Solius runs it on 1.1K.
  4. Price a membership, not a transaction. $99.99/year, "a limited number available to early adopters." CytoKind sells one $2,000 device and then has no second sale — while bulbs, re-authorisation, adherence support and coaching are all already happening, unpaid.
  5. Name the category you are not. Solius spends a whole post on why red-light panels and SAD lamps do not do this. CytoKind has never once drawn the line between a medical NB-UVB device and the cheap lamp the prospect finds while comparison-shopping.
  6. Attack the belief, not the objection. Their strongest content does not defend the product — it dismantles "sun is dangerous" with a 75:1 figure. CytoKind's equivalent belief to attack is "insurance won't cover this." It is false, and nobody is saying so.
  7. ⚠️ The one thing not to steal: the OTC route. Solius escaped the prescription by selling cash under a new product code. CytoKind cannot — insurance reimbursement requires the clinical notes, which requires the prescriber. That constraint is exactly why Solius is the more dangerous of the two competitors, and it is not a marketing problem.

What the other two prove about this one
  1. The distribution advantage is real and it is CytoKind's alone. Seven ads against zero and zero. Nobody in this category is bidding against you. The scarce thing is not budget — it is a second creative idea.
  2. The insurance lane is unoccupied. Phothera mentions $0 out-of-pocket once in ninety days and then defers to the dermatologist. Solius cannot mention it at all — it sells cash, over the counter. All six CytoKind sales came from the one story neither competitor is telling.
  3. The prescription stage is the structural risk, not a funnel bug. 18 parked prescriptions and 4 forms ever is not a copy problem. Solius removed the stage by inventing a product code. That option is closed to CytoKind while insurance requires clinical notes — which means the stage has to be operated, not optimised.
  4. Content volume is a choice, not a budget. 26 posts in nine weeks at Solius, zero dollars. 5 originals in ninety days at CytoKind, with money in the account.
  5. Nobody in this category has an audience. 243, 624, 1.1K. The one large owned audience in the whole teardown is Phothera's 25.9K Facebook page — and Phothera does not post to it.
