Five owners who hold their own dirt

Every line below is taken from a named public record — Dallas Central Appraisal District, Texas franchise-tax registration, or the company’s own published page. Where a fact could not be confirmed, it is marked. Nothing here is estimated and nothing is inferred.

01The filter

The operating company has to be the recorded owner of its own real estate.

That single test is what separates a name on a list from an owner who can actually sign. When the company owns the building, one decision moves the business, the land and the equipment together. When the family owns the land personally and the company only rents it, the transaction needs two agreements, two sets of expectations and two sets of heirs — and it usually needs a year longer.

Manda Machine Company, from the earlier set, is the clean counter-example: the sons run the company, the parents hold the dirt personally. Good business, wrong structure. It is not on this list for that reason alone.

Source universe: 239 Dallas County entities in the scored registry. Taken to the county property record: 17. Cleared the ownership test: 5. That is roughly one in five, and it is why the list is five and not twenty-five.
How to read a Dallas CAD account

A COMMERCIAL account is real property — the land and the building. A BPP account is Business Personal Property — the machines, the tooling, the inventory. A company with a BPP account and no commercial account is a tenant in its own plant. All five below hold both, in the operating company’s own name.

06What I would do with this list

  • Thermo Sensors first. Cleanest structure, smallest transaction, named principal, published desk lines. It is the fastest one to learn on.
  • Garland Steel by phone, not by email. No published address, and an $85,000 annual tax bill gives you an opening line that is about his money, not yours.
  • Republic and Cary are the two real prizes — $11.4M and $2.8M of equipment respectively, both with the next generation already seated. Those are conversations to open properly, not quickly.
  • Southern Maid is the one to price carefully. A licence book and a trademark with 89 years and an Elvis endorsement behind it does not value like a warehouse, and the wrong broker will treat it like one.
