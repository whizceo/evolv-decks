Contact and settings

Eight settings, checked from his own account.

  1. Contact email and phone
    Contact info → add issac.newton@expcommercial.com and 214-836-0135 (both already public in his listing posts). Set email visibility to the widest option offered. An owner who has decided to write does not want a form.
  2. The three website slots
    Replace masource.com and ibba.org (already shown under Organizations) with: the BizBuySell broker profile, eXp Commercial's IABS form, and TREC's Consumer Protection Notice. The last two are the TREC links (part eight).
  3. Public profile: everything visible
    Settings → Visibility → Edit your public profile. Visibility on, every section shown. An owner who Googles his name after an email is usually not signed in to LinkedIn; this is the version he reads.
  4. Follow stays secondary
    Settings → Visibility → Followers → “Make follow primary” off. The outbound lane needs owners to see Connect, not Follow.
  5. Profile viewing: name shown
    Settings → Visibility → Profile viewing options → name and headline. The profile visit in the outbound sequence is only a signal if the owner can see who looked.
  6. No connected tools
    Settings → Data privacy → remove any scheduler, automation or scraping app, and any browser extension acting on LinkedIn. This includes HeyReach until week 17. Pre-flight rule 06: one automated artefact on this profile undoes the whole strategy.
  7. Two-step verification on, one person logs in
    Nobody at Evolv logs into the account. A sign-in from Milan or from an assistant's laptop is the quickest way to get the account restricted, and during the test the account is the instrument.
  8. Audience baseline, today
    Analytics → Followers: screenshot job seniority, industry and location before anything changes. It becomes week 0 in the tracker, so the Owner Reach numbers have something to be compared with.

Settings are read from Issac's side only. This audit was run from a connected account, which cannot see his settings, so items three to eight are to be checked, not assumed wrong. Menu paths are LinkedIn desktop as of September 2026.

Part eight

TREC on LinkedIn

The profile and posts are advertisements under Texas rules. Four things keep them compliant.

  1. Broker name on the profile
    Rule 535.155 lets the broker's name for a social media post sit on the profile page, if it is readily noticeable and one click away. “eXp Commercial” in the headline does that for every post and message.
  2. IABS and Consumer Protection Notice links
    Rules 531.18 and 531.20: when social media is used for business, the profile, or a page it links to directly, carries both links. The contact-info website slots are the place (part seven).
  3. Team name only next to the broker
    “We Sell Group” is a team name (rule 535.154). It never appears alone, and the broker's name is at least half its size. It stays off the profile until the exact broker string is confirmed. That same string is what the email signatures are waiting for.
  4. “Broker” only if the license says broker
    His license entry reads “Real Estate Broker/Sales Agent”. The headline options use the word that matches his TREC license type; Issac confirms which it is.

Source: TREC, What You Need to Know to Comply With Our Social Media Rules. This is a reading of the published rules, not legal advice. eXp Commercial's compliance team has the final word.

Part nine

Two audiences, one profile

The owners he sells for, and the brokers his AI agency sells to, separated for sixteen weeks.

Broker Social Solutions, his AI agency, is a real business with a real audience: brokers who want prospecting systems. Its proof is automation. The owner Issac is selling to wants the opposite proof: that a person, not a system, is on the other side.

One profile cannot give both proofs at once. During the sixteen weeks of the hook test and the email lane, his personal profile carries only the owner story, and Broker Social Solutions lives on its own company page.

What that means in practice

  1. Headline and Featured
    No mention of automation, systems or Broker Social Solutions (covered in parts three and five).
  2. No experience entry yet
    Adding Broker Social Solutions as a current role would bring the automation story back to the top of the profile. It gets its entry in week 17.
  3. Company page posts stay on the company page
    No resharing Broker Social Solutions or AI news to his personal feed during the test.
  4. Fix the company page location
    It says San Francisco. The company is his, and he is in Dallas.

The cost, stated plainly: Broker Social Solutions loses the reach of his 21,646 followers for sixteen weeks. That is Issac's decision to make. Our recommendation is to pay it, because one listing signed with an owner is worth more than any amount of reach for the automation offer, and because the automation offer will have a stronger case in week 17 with a working pipeline to point to.

Part ten

Outbound in parallel

How LinkedIn outreach runs next to the hook test without breaking pre-flight rules 06 and 07.

The hook-test pre-flight has two rules that look like they forbid LinkedIn outbound. Rule 06: no connection-request automation, no auto-DM, no scheduler. Rule 07: no mass-connecting before or during the test, because changing who he is connected to changes who LinkedIn shows the posts to, and that changes the instrument.

Neither rule forbids an owner receiving a connection request from Issac. They forbid a machine sending it, and they forbid the volume changing mid-test. So the outbound lane runs on six rules:

  1. By hand, from his own account
    Issac sends every request and writes every message himself, on his own device. No tool sends anything. Rule 06 stands untouched.
  2. A fixed number, every weekday
    15 requests a weekday, 75 a week, from the week before post 01 to week 16. The same number every week makes it a constant in the measurement instead of a change between weeks. LinkedIn does not publish its weekly limit; third-party tests put it near 100 a week for a standard account, so 75 leaves room.
  3. Owners on the email list, and nobody else
    Only owners already on the prospect list, matched to their LinkedIn profile. No brokers, no agents, no vendors, no open networkers. Those are the people the posts are trying to reach anyway.
  4. No note, no sequence
    The request goes without a note. A note is a pitch sent before there is any trust. After an owner accepts, Issac writes one short message himself: no link, no attachment, no call request. Nothing is pre-written as a follow-up.
  5. Withdraw what is not accepted
    Requests still pending after 21 days are withdrawn. A growing pile of pending requests is one of the signals LinkedIn uses to tighten limits.
  6. One reply stops the other channel
    An owner who replies by email is removed from the LinkedIn sheet, and the other way round. Nobody is worked on two channels after they have answered.
